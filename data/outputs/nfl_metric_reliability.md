# Every team metric this lab can compute, screened

62 metric-and-side combinations across 2018-2025, from play-by-play and Next Gen Stats. Each is the club's weighted rate for that week, screened by the one question that governs a prior-season feature: **does it describe the same club a year later?**

**13 carry at 0.40 or better. 9 are close to noise.** Ranking is not endorsement — a metric that persists is one the market has had every year to price, and six features built from these same feeds have already been tested against the closing line and beaten none of it. This screen removes candidates; it promotes nothing.

## The same measurement, taken on each side of the ball

**21 of 24 metrics carry better on offence than on defence** — mean +0.381 against +0.244, a median gap of +0.155. This is the strongest structure in the screen, it is not an artefact of any one statistic, and it reproduces from a different direction the published finding that offensive EPA is stickier than defensive EPA.

**The three exceptions say what a defence actually controls**: stuff rate, havoc rate and the air yards it concedes all carry better on defence than offence. A defence chooses whether to sell out against the run and whether to force the ball short; it does not choose whether the quarterback it faces is accurate.

**Sack rate is the sharpest warning here.** A defence's sack rate carries at +0.132 — close to noise — while the pressure rate underneath it carries at +0.439 for a club and +0.884 for an individual rusher. Sacks are a noisy subset of pressures and a preview quoting last year's sack total is quoting the noise rather than the signal. That reproduces a published result this lab did not set out to test.

A club-season needs 8 games' worth of whatever the metric is measured over — expressed as a multiple of the median club-week, because a rate over plays and a rate over drives are not comparable in absolute terms.
